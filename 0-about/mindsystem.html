What It's Like to Work With Me

or, in simpler terms: how I approach puzzles, problems, and life in general

The Context

For years, I've tried to explain how I approach problems: why I ask certain questions, how I build systems, what I notice that others might miss, and the explanations never quite worked in words alone.

This diagram maps my working style. It's the result of years of introspective work, data gathering, and analysis across my personal systems and professional practice. Each component represents a real operational function.

At the center is the Care Core, the organizing principle that drives everything. Every system I build, every question I ask, every tool I create exists to improve outcomes for the people inside it.

What This Means in Practice

What I’m Like in a Corporate Environment

In a corporate environment, I often become the person people bring complicated problems to. I stay calm when priorities change. I can work independently without losing sight of the broader goal.

I ask questions early. I document decisions and create structure when ownership or information is unclear. When technical and non-technical teams need to work together, I help them understand each other and decide what happens next.

As a leader, I’m direct but supportive. I set clear expectations, share context, and listen carefully. I prefer helping people work through problems over simply handing them instructions.

I care about the quality of the outcome. I also care about the experience of the people producing it. A system that works at the expense of the people inside it is not a particularly good system.
